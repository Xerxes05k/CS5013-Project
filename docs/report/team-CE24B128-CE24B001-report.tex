\section{Executive summary}

Jamuna Hostel lends a small pool of cycles through a paper register at the gate. Residents cannot see which cycles are free, the 24-hour and 3-per-week limits are not enforced, and a broken cycle is simply handed to the next person.

We shipped a Java/Spring Boot web app (Thymeleaf, SQLite) that a resident uses on their own phone. They log in, pick a free cycle, and book it after OCR reads their ID card. At the stand they type their passcode while the guard watches; the guard has no device. At return they tick the parts the guard found broken, and a broken critical part sends the cycle to repair. The GS sees weekly usage hours and a maintenance list with a Repaired button. There are 126 automated tests. Scope change: the OpenNLP comment classifier from the proposal was dropped. Mid-demo feedback asked for a cycle's properties to decide its state, so per-part condition replaced it.

\section{Known limitations}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item \textbf{OCR on cards other than the one we tested.} Real-card evidence is one IIT Madras card (9 of 10 photos read) plus 18 synthetic cards. Under glare or on another layout the scan fails, and the resident falls back to typing their roll number after 3 tries. OCR also once read an 8 as a 5 at 95+ confidence. The exact match with the login prevents a wrong booking, but it shows up as a false rejection.
  \item \textbf{Safety rules that rely on SQLite's single connection.} ``One open rental per resident'' is enforced in the service, not the database. It holds only because the pool has one connection, and enlarging the pool would let two simultaneous bookings by the same resident through. SQLite foreign keys are also off, so hand-written SQL can leave orphan rows.
  \item \textbf{Resident comments raise no flags.} With the classifier cut, a comment like ``brakes feel weak'' reaches the GS only as text. The cycle stays bookable until a guard marks the part broken at a return.
\end{itemize}
